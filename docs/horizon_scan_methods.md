# AI capability and adoption horizon-scan method

Track B is separate from the 150-posting denominator. The evidence register
records complete-system identity, task and environment, tools/context/horizon,
success and high-reliability metrics, assistance, cost, contamination controls,
reproducibility, limitations, funding/conflicts, and assessment. Capability is
not adoption: adoption signals occupy a separate register and retain study-type
and sampling limitations.

Seven dimensions are independently scored 0–2: task representativeness,
evaluation independence, contamination controls, high-reliability reporting,
system/config disclosure, reproducible artifacts, and scientific-software
relevance. Scores 11–14 are strong, 7–10 moderate, and 0–6 weak. Weak evidence
may only enter a labeled watch list and cannot drive the central projection.
Sources must be refreshed monthly; leaderboards are inputs rather than forecasts,
standards are governance evidence rather than capability measures, and vendor
reports remain first-party evidence.
